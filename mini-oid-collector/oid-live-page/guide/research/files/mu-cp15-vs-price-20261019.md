# Does the 15s call/put buy ratio predict price? MU + CRWV, Mon 2026-10-19

Research only. No production code, deploys, restarts or trades. Keys came from the box env and were never printed.

## Verdict
**The 15s C/P tracks price as it moves, but it does not predict the next move.** On both MU and CRWV, the ratio lines up with the
*same* minute's price move (correlation about 0.4 to 0.6). It has essentially zero correlation with the *next* minute
(MU -0.01, CRWV -0.01 over the full session). Lead/lag shows the link sits at lag 0 and at the 15s *before* the reading, so price moves
first or at the same time, and the ratio reacts. No positive lag is above |0.03| on the full session.
Hit rates beat the base rate by only about 2 points (MU: ratio>1 then next minute up 48% vs a 46% base; ratio<1 then down 56% vs 54%).
That's inside the noise, and it's no better than simply using the last minute's price move.
What Jeffrey saw on MU is real, but it's confirmation, not a lead: when MU rises, people buy calls (and calls tick up), so the ratio
goes above 1 while the stock is rising and flips as it falls.

CRWV is the counterexample, and it's mostly a **thin-data artifact**: the median 15s window has 15 prints, with ~$1.2k of call buys and ~$2.2k of put buys.
In 15% of windows the put-buy side is exactly $0, and 21% of windows read >= 10. In those >=10 windows the median is about 5 call-buy prints, ~$4.7k, against **$0** of put buys,
so the "ratio" is really dividing by nothing. The next minute was up only 47% of the time in those windows (base 44%), and since 11:12 PT only 3 of 10.

## Method
- Data: Alpaca historical OPRA option **trades** (`/v1beta1/options/trades`) and SIP stock trades, 06:30 PT on. **Alpaca has no historical
  option quotes** (`/v1beta1/options/quotes` returns 404), so optflow's quote rule could not be replayed. Trades were sided with the
  **tick rule** per contract (uptick = buy, downtick = sell, zero tick carries the last side). Condition filters match optflow.py exactly
  (cancel/late `A-H`, multi-leg `fghijlmtu`, stock-tied `knopqrs` excluded; 9% of MU prints excluded).
- Contract sets: the live optflow set as of 11:31 / 11:41 PT (MU 54 contracts, CRWV 18) = `subs54`/`subs18`, plus a **broad** set
  (all expiries 10/19 to 11/09, strikes within ±12%: MU 662 contracts, CRWV 102). Results are nearly identical, and the table uses the live set.
- 15s windows sampled every 15s. Ratio = call-buy $ / put-buy $. L = ln((cb+k)/(pb+k)), k = max(2000, 5% of cb+pb). Thin = fewer than 5 prints (excluded, as on the chip).
- Price = last regular SIP trade at each stamp. Returns are in bp. 1-min return = 4 stamps.
- Effective N uses Bartlett's correction for autocorrelation of both series, and p-values use effective N. A non-overlapping (every 4th stamp) check gives the same answer.

## Sanity check vs the live stream (11:38 to 11:44 PT, MU)
Polled `/v1/optflow` + `/v1/recent?sym=MU` read-only every ~15s (`validate.py`, `validate.json`):
- Per-trade side agreement, tick rule vs live quote rule: **77%** (810 trades; 81% on at-ask/at-bid prints, ~68% on inside-spread prints).
- Live 15s chip vs reconstruction at the same instant: correlation of log ratio **0.71**, same side of 1 in **79%** of 19 samples. Median cb $33.6k live vs $36.2k reconstructed, and pb $9.0k vs $8.0k.
  So the reconstruction is a fair but noisy proxy for the chip.

## Which metric generalizes better?
None of them predicts the next minute on either name: every next-minute correlation is within ±0.1 on the full session. For a **coincident** read:
- Raw ratio: works on MU but blows up on CRWV when pb is about $0 (it shows 99 or 10 to 40 on 1 to 5 tiny prints).
- Smoothed L: same-minute 0.51 MU / 0.42 CRWV. It tames the divide-by-zero.
- Net $ (cb-pb): best on MU (0.56) but weak on CRWV (0.27), because a single print dominates and dollar scale isn't comparable across names.
- **Normalized net aggressor** ((cb-cs)-(pb-ps)) / total classified $, bounded -1..+1: 0.45 MU / 0.44 CRWV, the most consistent across the two names.
Recommendation if the chip is ever changed: show L or the normalized net, require a minimum dollar floor (e.g. >= $5k classified and >= 3 buy
prints on *each* side before showing a ratio >= 10), and keep it labeled as confirmation rather than a signal.

## Caveats
- One session and two names. MU's full session gives ~1,250 stamps (effective N ~600). "Since 11:12" is only ~30 min (effective N ~58), which is too little
  to say anything on its own. The short-window CRWV figures (e.g. ratio>1 then next up 27%) reflect a downtrend, not a signal.
- The tick rule is mechanically tied to the underlying (calls uptick when the stock upticks), which inflates the *same-minute* correlation. Part of
  the live chip's same-minute link is also mechanical. This doesn't help prediction, which is ~0 either way.
- Opening vs closing can't be known from prints, so a "call buy" may be a short covering. Contract sets drift every 15 min live, and here they're fixed.

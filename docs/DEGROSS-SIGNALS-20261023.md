## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Nothing predicts the day a de-gross ends.** Every published study of deleveraging episodes (August 2007 quant unwind, 2008, momentum crashes, liquidity spirals) finds the end is recognizable on the day it happens, from the reversal itself, not the day before. Prime-broker "pace" data arrives one to three days late and no house publishes an "X percent done" figure for this episode.
2. **What has documented same-day edge is small and index-level:** the first-30-minute return of the index predicts the last-30-minute return in the same direction, strongest on high-volatility days and when dealers are short gamma (Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, JFE 2018; Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, JFE 2021, UNVERIFIED from memory). That is a last-hour lean whose magnitude is not established in anything read for this note, not an entry signal at 10:05.
3. **The de-gross metrics are a regime switch for this desk, not a trigger.** They decide which side has the better base rate (calls on a confirmed down-open reversal in late innings; puts on continuation in early innings), size, hold time, and whether to fade the open. The desk's own measured conditioner (1-minute relative volume at or above 3x) stays the entry.
4. **Where the episode stands (EXTRACT, see section 3):** gross is cut and shorts are crowded (Goldman: month-to-date short sales about 5x long buys, short leverage at the 99th percentile of five years, "short squeeze risk alert?"), but tech is still about 23.5 percent of the net book, the 90th percentile of five years. That is a two-sided tape: squeezes up on any catalyst, long liquidation restarting on any capex or financing headline. Thursday 10/22 was the second kind.
5. **Thursday's signature was a sector repricing, not a systemic de-gross:** semis down 6 to 7 percent with all 30 SOX members red, while NYSE breadth was flat, the 10-year yield fell below 5 percent, and VIX's intraday high (20.3) stayed below its 10/16 high (22.3). Market-wide capitulation tools will not fire for this episode; the internals have to be computed on the desk's own 144-name universe, and the vol thermometer has to be VXN and single-name semis vol, not VIX: VIX/VIX3M stayed in contango (about 0.95) through a -6.7 percent SOX day, while VXN/VIX had risen to 1.46 by 10/20 to 10/21, its episode high.
6. **The one same-day measurement of the selling that exists for free is the Korea Exchange's investor-type flow.** Goldman named Korea and Taiwan the epicentre. Foreign net selling of KOSPI ran about 1T won a day in early October, was only 113B won on Thursday's -3.5 percent session, and about 145B won by 10:00 Friday morning Seoul time, absorbed by domestic institutions; foreigners had net bought Samsung for four sessions through 10/21. By the note's own logic the Asia leg reads "slowing". Taiwan is closed Friday for a typhoon, so its catch-up to Thursday lands Monday.
7. **Friday is the quietest scheduled day of the fortnight** (flash PMIs 9:45 ET, UMich final 10:00 ET, weekly expiry, Fed blackout, no large-cap tech earnings). Selling on no news is positioning; a bounce on no news into expiry is short covering into thin corporate bid. The next hard positioning read is Goldman's weekly on Monday 10/26. The real event cluster is 10/28 to 10/30 (FOMC, MSFT and GOOGL, GDP and PCE, AMZN and INTC, BoJ, month-end).

**What the owner's credit page can and cannot do.** The page (FRED HY and IG OAS with 10-year percentiles, HYG day) is a regime card with a one-day lag compounded by the desk's cache. It can tell you whether the episode is a positioning event (HY within a few tens of bp of its start, bonds rallying on the worst day) or a funding event (HY wider by 100 bp or more with yields rising). It cannot tell you anything about the next hour. Two cheap upgrades: replace the HYG day input with HYG minus IEI (duration stripped) and HYGH mid, and regress next-day FRED change on same-day HYG minus IEI over the 10-year history the tape already pulls, to get a nowcast OAS tile with its own error bar.

## 6. How this maps onto the desk, and the hazards

**Which setups fit a de-gross tape.** Continuation re-entry (A2) fits negative-gamma trend days, which 10/14, 10/16 and 10/22 all were. Surge-at-low reversal fits only after the confirmation above; 10/15 was the one such day and it did not hold. Sector sympathy (C) is mechanically more frequent because the universe trades as one factor (all 30 SOX members down on 10/22) and is also more crowded. The opening-range setup (F) should be evaluated separately on 10/14 to 10/22 data before it is traded in this regime: it sits where IV crush and spread cost are both maximal.

**The options-buyer hazards (MODEL rows are Black-Scholes arithmetic, UNVERIFIED as market facts).**
- Implied-volatility crush on a relief day. An at-the-money call with two trading days left, held two hours: if the stock rises 2 percent and IV holds at 80, the call makes about +32 percent; if IV falls from 80 to 55 on the same move, about +5 percent; if the stock is flat and IV falls 80 to 55, about -37 percent. The desk's thesis that a 0.3 to 0.8 percent move becomes +10 to 20 percent on premium holds only when IV is stable. Single-name semis IV can fall 15 to 25 points on the first relief day (UNVERIFIED rule of thumb). The existing filter, no call buying at an IV rank above 80 against the trailing 60 sessions (LOCAL), is the right defence and will exclude most of the universe this week, which is the point.
- Theta is IV-independent in percentage terms: a 0DTE at-the-money call bought at 10:00 ET loses about 9.6 percent an hour with the stock unchanged at any IV. A 45-minute time stop costs about 7 percent of premium before direction is even wrong.
- Spreads: 5 to 15 percent of mid on 2 to 5 DTE contracts in second-tier semis in stress (UNVERIFIED). A 10 percent round trip makes a +10 percent target a break-even. Log spread at entry on every candidate and look at the 10/14 to 10/22 distribution before anything else.
- Gaps: META -7 percent, LRCX -11.8 percent and AMD -8 percent on 10/22 were largely overnight repricing printed in the opening auction, where spreads are widest and 1-minute norms are least meaningful.
- Correlation goes to one: selection edge collapses toward index direction while you pay single-name spreads and single-name IV.
- Range: with 1-day one-sigma moves of 3.8 percent at 60 IV and 5 percent at 80 IV, a +12 / -20 / 45-minute bracket on premium is hit faster both ways; the per-ticket win rate may not change, but variance and the chance of several same-direction losses in minutes rise.

**Risk rules for the week (UNVERIFIED, consistent with the desk's existing gates).** Size off the realized range, not the usual notional: a VIX of 19 is about a 1.2 percent SPX daily sigma, SMH's is several times that. Halve the ticket count, not just the size. Keep the IV-rank gate and the spread gate on. Time stops shorter than usual, because theta per hour is the same and the moves are faster. No tickets inside the event windows. No adds into the last hour against a sell imbalance.

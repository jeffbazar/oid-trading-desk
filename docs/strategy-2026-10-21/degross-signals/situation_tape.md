### 4. What the sequence says about the de-grossing (UNVERIFIED synthesis of EXTRACT facts)

1. Peak forced selling was **Wed Oct 14** (Goldman: heaviest de-grossing day; SOX −4.3% closing near lows; late-day acceleration). **Thu Oct 15** showed the classic exhaustion signature: half the de-grossing volume, SOX −4.4% intraday reversed to −0.9%, CRWV reversed from 52-wk low, Dow +556 (rotation, not liquidation). **Fri Oct 16** was a second leg (−3.83% SOX, VIX 22.31 intraday → 19.99 close) with broad long liquidation in memory/AI-debt names.
2. The Oct 19–21 bounce was narrow (RSP down, decliners 2:1 on Oct 19; intraday SOX gains faded), i.e., short-covering/opportunistic buying in the most-sold names rather than re-grossing. No press report of hedge-fund net *buying* of tech for Oct 19–21 was found.
3. Oct 22 broke the Oct 16 Nasdaq and SOX closing lows on a fresh catalyst mix (capex from Meta/Tesla, Lam guide, oil/Iran) and the 10Y fell 17bp — behaviour consistent with renewed de-risking rather than with JPM's "largely unwound" call; Goldman's own 90th-percentile tech allocation says the book was still crowded going in.
4. Data-availability for the desk: VIX/VIX term structure, SOX, RSP−SPY, 10Y, Brent are real-time free; Goldman/JPM/MS prime-broker flow reads are client-only, T+1 for clients and typically T+2 to T+4 when they reach press (the Oct 15 data hit Bloomberg Oct 19); S3 short-interest data is paid/daily; CoreWeave/Oracle CDS is Bloomberg/Markit paid intraday; CME FedWatch is free intraday; CPI/FOMC are calendar (next FOMC Oct 27–28, midterms Nov 3).

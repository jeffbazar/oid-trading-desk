# OID: One Plan for Scaling a Discretionary Options Desk into Supervised Bots

*Synthesis of five proposals and three judge panels, 2026-10-21. Spine: the execution-risk proposal (ranked first by all three panels), with the must-keep ideas from the systematic-PM, data-engineer, ML-scientist and skeptic-product proposals merged in. Every number traces to the desk maps, the live-page studies dated 10/20, the Oct-4 paper ledger, or the vendor fact sheet; vendor and regulatory facts the fact sheet could not open are marked UNVERIFIED inline. All clocks in this document are Pacific Time; regular trading hours are 6:30 AM–1:00 PM PT.*

## Executive summary

- **The edge to scale is Jeffrey's hand, and it is unrecorded.** His live fills are in neither repo; the measured systems (a long-calls-only paper desk with 24 h median holds, and page scores that are near coin-flip) are not the thing that works. Capture comes before any automation, and costs $0.
- **The only conditioner that survives every study is 1-minute rvol ≥ 3×** (15-min breakout odds 8.7% → 26.7% away from walls, 82,190 ticker-minutes). Walls are noise at the single-name level (46.9% vs 44.9%). The live C/P flow input appears to hurt (±2: 50.3% → 55.8% without it). Holding a score 30 s–5 min before acting helps. All of this is one or two correlated sessions.
- **Automate in this order: capture → exits → candidate ranking → entries.** Exits are deterministic and are where the paper desk demonstrably failed (43 of 57 closes were batch administrative events; AMZN 260C bled to −61%; GOOGL 340C gave back +164% MFE to +17.8%). Entries, where the human edge lives, are automated last.
- **No LLM ever touches an order.** A deterministic Execution Gateway on the data mini is the only process with broker credentials; any intent that did not originate from a human click or a frozen, hash-pinned policy is rejected by construction. Rose and Hal become proposers and narrators.
- **Alpaca paper now, Alpaca live first; Robinhood is out of the order path.** The Robinhood Agentic MCP was stocks-only at its May 2026 launch (UNVERIFIED) and desk policy already prohibits orders there. Tradier is evaluated only if emulated brackets prove unreliable; its sandbox is 15-min delayed with no streaming, so it cannot be paper-tested at seconds scale (UNVERIFIED).
- **The target variable changes.** Not "direction right at 5 m" but the triple-barrier option-leg label: did the contract's bid reach +X% over the entry ask before a −Y% stop or a time stop. That label needs the streamed OPRA BBO archive (R2) that does not exist yet; starting it is a day-1 item.
- **Gates are measured in events, not days.** Detecting a 50% → 55% lift needs ~785 independent events; with overlapping 15 s snapshots and correlated names the design effect is 3–5×. Promotion needs ≥ 800 events and ≥ 15 sessions, Brier skill ≥ 0.01 vs the surge-only baseline, and executable expectancy with a session-block bootstrap CI excluding zero.
- **First live envelope is tiny and raised one notch per 20 clean sessions:** 1 contract, $500 debit, 2 open seats, $750 daily loss stop, 15 s veto window. Autonomous entries are a 2027 question, for one setup only.
- **Buy nothing new.** No FlashAlpha Alpha ($1,499/mo), no DRAMeXchange, no Silicon Data, no Massive options, no X top-up. Spend is $0 beyond today's run rate until a registered study names the purchase; the one likely add is a targeted Databento pull capped at $300 after a cost query.
- **Day-1 owner decisions:** open the research-mini network path and set the seal date D; approve the 260-contract OPRA subscription set; commit to clicking Call/Put/Pass/Watch on every decision; approve Alpaca paper as the execution target; confirm the broker's PDT/intraday-margin regime in writing.

## 1. Diagnosis

**What the edge is today.** Jeffrey reads a genuine volume event on a liquid AI/semis name, commits to a direction in seconds, buys a near-ATM option so that a 0.3–0.8% underlying move becomes +10–20% on the premium, and exits before the move decays. That is a reaction-speed and exit-discipline edge on a high-gamma instrument, not a forecasting edge. He reports it has worked; nothing in either repository can confirm or deny it, because his live fills are in no repo and no decision row has ever been written at decision time (map-desk-ops f.10; DATA_GAP_REPORT row J: "no structured rejected/no-trade candidate records").

**Three uncoordinated systems.** (1) Jeffrey's hand, unmeasured. (2) The paper desk: Rose and Hal running wall-press and Soft A setups, long calls only (54/57 tickets, zero outright puts), median Rose hold 24 h, closed in administrative batches. (3) The live page: Direction/Light/Full scores whose own Research tab rates them near coin-flip. The bots' Soft A workflow and the page's scores do not reference each other (map-desk-ops g, last bullet). The thing that reportedly makes money has no data trail; the things that have data trails are not the thing that makes money.

**What the 10/20 studies say, read plainly.** Every "all nonzero" hit rate is below 50%: 34% (direction first look), 38% (core waiting), 38.8% (light replay), 41.8% (C/P agreement), 47.6% (drop-one baseline). The most extreme core reading (±3) gets worse with a 30 s wait (34.8% → 22.2%, n = 27). The score is right most often when momentum is decelerating (46.7%) and worst when accelerating (31.3%): it saturates after the move and chases. The raw direction score correlated −0.39 with close return on its one live day, which is one or two effective observations of market direction, not an inverted signal. The +/− asymmetry in calibration (core +2.0 raw → 58%, −2.5 raw → 33%) is an up-day base rate; no same-window base rate is reported anywhere.

The positive cells share one conditioner. ±2 held ≥ 30 s went 37% → 43.2% (n = 782); ±2 with a volume surge held 2 min: 61.5% (n = 26); light ±3 with surge held 5 min: 67.5% (n = 40); wall-magnet study: a 1-min rvol ≥ 3× roughly triples 15-min breakout odds away from walls (8.7% → 26.7%). Binomial 95% intervals: n = 40 at 67.5% is roughly 51–81%; n = 26 at 61.5% is roughly 41–80%. Fifteen-second snapshots labeled at 5 min overlap about 20×, so n = 14,703 is closer to n ≈ 700 effective, clustered on correlated names in one or two sessions. Under `program.md` §13–16 this is hypothesis generation; 10/19–10/20 are consumed development dates for any study of these cells.

Two further results matter for design. Removing the 15 s/1 m C/P flow input raised ±2 right-after-5-min from 50.3% to 55.8% (n 1,552 → 1,034) and cut score changes 31%: the input, with its small classified coverage, looks like churn. The wall-magnet study found walls add ~2 points over chance (46.9% vs 44.9% above the call wall; 9.2% vs 9.9% above the put wall), consistent with the academic literature, where dealer-gamma effects are index-level and regime-level (UNVERIFIED-3P, prior knowledge), not single-name levels.

**What the paper ledger says.** Net +$14,888 on $73,384 recycled debit, 32 W / 24 L / 1 flat, Sep 10–Oct 4. Rose's top three tickets (AMD 550C +$4,917.50, META 670C +$4,550, LRCX 310C +$1,975) are $11,442.50 of her +$12,844, or 89%. Forty-three of 57 closes were batch owner events on eight timestamps; all 26 Hal closes were batch; the 9/18 cohort of seven wall-presses closed together on 9/21, one market move counted seven times. Seven Hal entries are stamped 9:15 PM PT. Entry = ask, exit = mark, zero fees or slippage; two credit spreads carry negative cash yet are tagged `win_exit`; MFE is a 5–15-minute sampled max, so the +50% trail-arm has never been tested against an executable peak. The close-trigger engine has never auto-closed anything and produced 0 review alerts. Profit factor 7.7 is a function of three correlated tickets closed together; Hal's 1.75 with median +0.4% is noise. The desk's own audit is right that this sample supports no return target. It also cannot be a baseline for Jeffrey's style: wrong instruments, wrong horizon, wrong exits.

**Where it is fragile.**
1. Eleven decisions live only in Jeffrey's head (map-desk-ops f): go/no-go, gate waivers, which wall "governs", exit timing, loss acceptance, contract geometry under the cap, universe, vendor economics, rule promotion, macro discount, live authority. You cannot automate what is not written down.
2. No negatives. No WATCH/NO_TRADE/REJECT log exists, so no classifier can learn his selection and no detector can be scored for precision.
3. No option-leg label. Options history (family C) is ABSENT; every study is `UNDERLYING_ONLY`; "did the option bid reach +10% over my entry ask" has never been computed for a single event.
4. Latency unmeasured. `latency_model_ref` is `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED` everywhere; the collector `LagHistogram` is not persisted; the LLM agent path on the box is seconds to minutes while Jeffrey's holds start at seconds.
5. No archive the research sandbox can reach. The data mini now holds 1-min bars, 1,045 wall snapshots, a 15 s `score_features` table and nightly `score_labels`, but none of it is in git, hashed, or reachable by the research mini, which has users, budget and isolation checks but zero datasets and no network path (the data mini silently drops traffic from 192.168.5.199).
6. One regime: Sep 10–Oct 20, an up-tape in AI names. Puts have never been traded in paper.
7. Governance drift: two Macro score definitions coexist; "chat approval ≠ ticket" is printed while a chat "yes" is in practice the trigger; the X pool is at $55.72.

**Working thesis.** The edge to encode is *surge → fast gamma entry → mechanical exit*, with Jeffrey's selection judgment learned from his own logged decisions. Scores stay in shadow until they beat the surge-only rule on sealed future sessions. Walls are a diagnostic feature, never a trigger.

## 2. Scaling strategy

Four stages. Each stage adds authority only after a gate measured by code Jeffrey does not edit, on data that post-dates the rule, in event counts not day counts. Every stage keeps the previous stage's kill switches.

**Stage 0: Discretionary with capture (now → ~day 30).** Jeffrey trades exactly as today. Every Call/Put/Pass/Watch and every exit is written at decision time, keyed to the 15 s `score_features` snapshot id. His live fills are exported daily and joined. The Exit Manager runs in paper shadow against his entries, so the first bracket counterfactuals exist from week two. Gate to leave: ≥ 20 sessions; ≥ 150 decision rows including ≥ 50 Pass/Watch negatives; ≥ 60 live fills with timestamps; ≥ 100 option-leg labels computed from R2; `decision_at → order_ack` latency histogram persisted; the 57 paper tickets restated with signed cashflows, fees and administrative closes separated.

**Stage 1: Assisted (~day 30 → 90).** A deterministic Candidate Ranker puts ≤ 5 names in a Now lane with the three study-backed conditioners. The ticket drawer is pre-filled with contract, debit, bracket, break-even win rate and gate results; the button says "Send to gateway (PAPER)". Jeffrey clicks; the Gateway sends to Alpaca paper and runs the synthetic bracket. A frozen Shadow Trader emits entries on the same snapshots. Gate: ≥ 20 sessions and ≥ 60 Gateway tickets (the ledger ran ~2 tickets/day, so the 100-ticket bar one proposal set would stall); slippage-adjusted net expectancy per ticket > 0 with a session-block bootstrap 90% CI excluding 0; top-3 share of net < 40%; lane precision (lane candidates acted on that hit the target before the stop) ≥ 45% with CI lower bound ≥ 35%, reported separately from Jeffrey's own hit rate on the same events; zero unreconciled fills; bracket exits dominate (administrative closes < 10%); kill switch drilled weekly with logged cancel-all latency.

**Stage 2: Supervised auto (~day 60–90 → 150).** The Candidate Engine sends paper orders itself; Jeffrey has a 15 s veto window on entries and none on exits. After 20 clean paper sessions, live with hard caps: 1 contract, $500 debit per ticket, 2 open seats, $750 daily loss stop. Gate to raise caps (one notch per 20 clean sessions: $500 → $1,500 → $5,000 debit; 2 → 4 → 8 seats): live expectancy after real fills and fees not statistically worse than paper, paired by session; realized slippage ≤ modeled; calibration ECE ≤ 0.05 on live labels; zero risk-limit breaches; reconciliation mismatch count 0.

**Stage 3: Autonomous inside an envelope (not before 2027).** For the one setup that passed Stage 2. Entries and exits auto within per-name, per-sector and daily caps; Jeffrey sets `policy.yaml` weekly and does not approve tickets. Any gate metric that fails on a rolling 20-session window demotes to Stage 2 automatically. The desk never becomes "fully autonomous" in the sense of unlimited; it becomes autonomous inside a config file the owner signs.

**Measured how, throughout.** One primary metric: net expectancy per eligible opportunity under `program.md` §18 fills (buy at ask after `decision_at` plus measured latency, sell at bid at exit plus latency), reported with B0/B1/B2 baselines, Brier skill for the probability component, top-3-contributor share, and a session-block bootstrap. Hit rate alone never gates anything.

**What gets automated first and why.** Capture, because it is lossless and free. Exits second, because they are deterministic, latency-tolerant, and the ledger's documented failure mode. Candidate ranking third, because it removes scanning cost over 209 tiles without taking authority. Entries last, because they are the only step whose error is unbounded and the only place the human edge lives.

**What never gets automated:** gate waivers, contract geometry under the debit cap (the OTM-substitution habit that produced MU 1600C at 46% OTM), universe changes, and anything on 0DTE.

Three planes with a hard boundary. The **data plane** and the **execution plane** are deterministic Python on the data mini, next to the Alpaca stream. The **reasoning plane** is the LLM agents on the box, which produce slow features, proposals and reviews, never orders. The box is a shared 8 vCPU host with 0.8–5.4 GiB free; it never holds broker credentials.

**Jeffrey owns `policy.yaml`.** Gates, thresholds, universe, budgets and caps live in one git-versioned file with a signed changelog. Bots read it; no bot writes it. Hard ceilings (debit $5,000, 15 seats, daily loss) are coded into the Gateway and `policy.yaml` can only lower them. Every override Jeffrey makes is a row in `overrides` with a reason chip, so waivers become data instead of chat sentences. This replaces the human changelog of promoted never_again rules and the "named gate governs" pattern.

**Order path contract.** An intent is a typed row: `order_intent {intent_id (uuid, idempotency key), ts_decision, snapshot_id, setup_id, policy_version, actor ∈ {jeffrey, engine:<hash>}, symbol, occ_symbol, side = buy_to_open, qty, limit_px, max_debit, bracket {tp_pct, stop_pct, time_stop_s}, risk_check_id, status}`. Companion tables: `risk_check {checks[] {name, value, limit, pass}, verdict ∈ {PASS, REJECT, HALT}}`, `fill {broker_order_id, ts_broker, px, qty, fee, slippage_vs_mid_at_decision}`, `position {peak_mark, bracket_state ∈ {OPEN, TP1_DONE, TRAILING, CLOSING}, time_stop_at}`, `recon_event {broker_qty, oms_qty, delta, action}`. SQLite WAL on the data mini, mirrored to Parquet nightly.

**Risk Sentinel checks, in order; any fail = REJECT; two REJECTs in a row on different intents = HALT.** (1) kill flag absent; (2) `actor` is a human click or a frozen policy hash in `policy.yaml` (the "LLM-touched" switch: anything else is rejected by construction); (3) mode ∈ {PAPER, LIVE} matches the intent; (4) `policy_version == current`; (5) quote age ≤ 5 s on both underlying and contract, from the stream; (6) spread/mid ≤ 5% core, ≤ 8% sector at half size; (7) debit ≤ cap and open debit + new ≤ book cap; (8) seats < cap; per-name 1; per-sector-group 2; Correlation Sentinel ≤ 3 correlated seats across groups; (9) no earnings inside the hold window and no scheduled macro print within 15 min, from the deterministic Event Gate file; (10) day P&L > −daily loss limit; (11) consecutive-loss pause (3 losses → 30 min); per-name cooldown 10 min after a stop; (12) order rate ≤ 6/min desk-wide; duplicate `intent_id` rejected; (13) underlying day% < 3% (the existing chase gate, kept until evidence says otherwise); (14) session clock 6:35 AM–12:50 PM PT for new entries; (15) measured latency budget: no policy may run whose target horizon is shorter than 5× the measured `decision_at → broker_ack` latency.

**Synthetic brackets.** Alpaca options support `simple` and `mleg` order classes only, with no bracket/OCO and no trailing stop (UNVERIFIED-S). The Bracket Manager therefore runs the bracket itself: on fill, post a resting GTC limit sell at the target (GTC on options is reported working per the 2026-08-28 changelog, UNVERIFIED-S; fall back to day orders re-posted each morning if not); a software stop evaluated on every BBO tick that cancels the resting take-profit and sends a marketable limit at bid − 1 tick, re-priced every 2 s up to five times, then market as last resort; a time stop that flattens at `time_stop_at`; a hard flat for everything at 12:55 PM PT. Stream disconnect > 10 s with open positions → flatten-at-limit sequence, then HALT. Reconnect → reconcile before any new send, so a double-fill cannot survive a reconnect.

**Kill switches, independent and drilled weekly.** (1) File flag `/opt/oid/KILL` on the data mini, checked before every send and every 1 s by the Bracket Manager: cancel all, flatten, refuse new; any bot or Data Ops can set it, only Jeffrey clears. (2) Daily loss limit and max-open-debit ceilings hard-coded in the Gateway. (3) Broker-side: Alpaca trading-key revocation script (keep the data key) and a close-all script runnable from Jeffrey's phone. (4) Feed health: Alpaca stream lag > 5 s or OPRA gap > 10 s → no new entries, exits continue; FlashAlpha quota < 200 → walls go diagnostic, no block. (5) Drift halt: today's realized hit rate on ≥ 20 decisions below the 10-session 5th percentile, or model p-distribution drift (KS test vs last 10 sessions) → shadow only. (6) Three consecutive fills worse than modeled by > 1 tick → halt entries. (7) Heartbeat: cockpit shows Gateway heartbeat age and goes red at 10 s. (8) Physical: launchd disable; the Gateway refuses to start without a fresh 24 h `arm` token. The weekly drill flips (1), verifies cancel-all latency < 2 s and zero residual orders, and logs it.

**Broker.** Robinhood stays Jeffrey's manual venue if he likes it. It is out of the bot order path: no public equities/options API; the Agentic MCP trades in a separate account and was stocks-only at launch with options "coming soon" (UNVERIFIED-3P, press); third parties report the customer agreement prohibits bots on equities/options (UNVERIFIED-3P). Alpaca first: the data pipeline already lives there, paper accounts get Level 3 automatically (UNVERIFIED-S), alpaca-py is verified, and the same symbols drive signals and fills. Confirm in writing before Stage 2: options order classes and TIF, no trailing stop, `mleg` support, the ~1,000 websocket contract cap, and Alpaca's post-June-2026 margin regime. Tradier (native OTOCO with option legs, $10/mo flat, UNVERIFIED-S) is the fallback if synthetic brackets fail a 20-session paper test; IBKR only if the book outgrows both.

## 4. Models

**Model the thing the exit rule pays on.** Two tracks, never confused (`program.md` §17).

- *Executable label (primary).* For a decision at `t0` on the nearest 0.50Δ 5–21 DTE call/put listed at `t0`: `L_opt(X, Y, T) ∈ {target, stop, timeout}` where target = contract **bid** reaches ≥ (1 + X) × entry **ask** before bid ≤ (1 − Y) × entry ask or `t0 + T` or 12:55 PM PT. Registered grid: X ∈ {10, 12, 15, 20}%, Y ∈ {15, 20, 25}%, T ∈ {20, 45, 90 min}. Secondary continuous label: `ret_policy`, the signed net return of each bracket under §18 fills and the registered latency. `L_exc`: 15-min maximum favorable excursion in ATR units from the true stream, not sampled. Because the label is bid-vs-entry-ask, the spread is already inside it: a 5%-wide quote needs a ~17% mid move to show +12%.
- *Underlying label (cheap, interim).* 5/15/30-min excess return vs QQQ; the existing `score_labels`, published as `UNDERLYING_ONLY` while the option archive is shallow.

**Baselines, always reported.** B0: same-name, same-session, same-time-of-day up-fraction. B1: QQQ h-minute return sign. B2: the naive surge rule, enter on every rvol ≥ 3× at day_high/day_low with no score. A model earns nothing until it beats B2.

**Features (≤ 30, all `feature_available_at ≤ decision_at`, enforced in code).** Tier 1 (some evidence): 1-min rvol vs same-minute norm, 5-min rvol, 10 s pace, surge flag/side/age, held-score seconds, score value and config hash, momentum state with hysteresis, distance to day high/low in ATR, sector-group rvol and % change, QQQ/SMH 5-min return, minutes since open. Tier 2 (option microstructure, new from R2/R1): ATM spread % of mid, ask size, option volume vs OI, IV change over 15 min, 15-min C/P premium with coverage %. Tier 3 (diagnostic): wall distance % and age, UW lean with age, news first-seen within 15 min (presence and tone), VIX 15-min change, macro ±2, index GEX sign from UW as a regime flag. Excluded: raw 15 s C/P at < 20% coverage, UW lean of unknown age, anything an LLM produced without a prompt hash.

**Model classes, in order; each earns its place on out-of-fold Brier skill.** (1) Stratified hit-rate tables with Wilson intervals: what the Research tab already does, made the registered B2/B3. (2) L2 logistic regression, as `config.draft.json` specifies, Platt-calibrated on the last 20% of each outer window. (3) `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (sklearn 1.9.1 is installed), max_depth 3, ≤ 200 trees, min_samples_leaf 50, monotone constraints on rvol and held-duration; admitted only if Brier skill over logistic ≥ 0.01 with the CI excluding 0. Nothing deeper: the independent-event count is in the hundreds. Walk-forward by session with purge by actual `label_end_at` and a ≥ 2-session embargo; session-block bootstrap; PBO reported once > 5 configs are compared.

**Calibration.** A trading threshold is set on calibrated probability and expected net dollars per opportunity, not on hit rate. Isotonic when n ≥ 2,000, Platt below. Reliability by decile with n is the first chart on the Research tab; a model is calibrated when the 10-session rolling bucket hit rate is within ±5 points of forecast for every bucket with n ≥ 50; ECE ≤ 0.05 is a live gate.

**Sample sizes, stated once so nobody argues later.** Detecting 50% → 55% at two-sided α 0.05 and power 0.8 needs ~785 independent events. With a 3–5× design effect from 15 s overlap and cross-name correlation, budget 2,500–4,000 snapshots or ~800 non-overlapping decision events. The "±2 held 30 s" cell produced ~780 holds on 10/20, so 10–15 sessions; the "±2 + surge held 2 min" cell produced 26, so 30–60 sessions; the option-leg label at 5–10 trades/day needs 50–100 sessions to separate 40% from 46%. The page's "beat the current score on 10+ days" plan needs an event floor, not just a day count.

## 7. Strategy and edge techniques

**What "consistent arbitrage" means here.** There is no arbitrage. There is a repeatable conditional edge: after a genuine volume event in a liquid name, short-horizon continuation is more likely than the base rate, and option convexity turns a 0.5–1% underlying move into +10–20% on a near-ATM contract. The edge is in event selection, speed, refusing to chase, and exiting by rule, traded identically every time so the law of large numbers works in weeks. The bid-ask spread is the tax that decides everything; retail options flow pays ~12.6% effective spread on cheap weeklies and loses on average (Bryzgalova et al., UNVERIFIED-S), which is why every setup carries a hard liquidity gate.

**The cost arithmetic that must hold before any setup goes live.** With the label defined on bid vs entry ask, a +X target and −Y stop give expected value `p·X − (1 − p)·Y − fees`, so the break-even hit rate is `Y / (X + Y)` plus a fee term. At +12/−20 that is 62.5% before fees; with $0.65/contract each way on a $2.00 option (0.65% round trip, broker fee UNVERIFIED) it is ~64.5%. The 61.5% and 67.5% headline cells therefore straddle break-even for that bracket, which is exactly why the bracket is chosen from the E5 grid and not asserted. At +15/−15 the bar is ~52%; at +20/−15 it is ~44%, but wider targets are reached less often, and timeouts land in between. The Contract Selector computes the per-ticket break-even from the live spread and fees and rejects any ticket whose break-even exceeds the calibrated probability. A proposal that said 40–50% precision is "profitable at 12/20 payoff" was wrong; it is a losing policy at any latency.

**Setups to register (all same-day, ATM 5–21 DTE, bracket from E5, interim shadow default +12 / −20 / 45 min).**

- **A. Surge continuation (primary; the rule to freeze now).** Trigger: 1-min rvol ≥ 3× same-minute norm at a new day_high (calls) or day_low (puts); 5-min rvol ≥ 2×; bar closes in the top/bottom 30% of its range; momentum state not ACCEL for ≥ 60 s (hysteresis; ACCEL is the worst state); underlying day% < 3%; QQQ 5-min return same sign or flat; no earnings within 24 h; no scheduled macro print within 15 min; 6:35–11:30 AM or 12:00–12:50 PM PT. Entry on the next 5 s bar after the triggering minute closes (`program.md` §18 forbids fills inside the triggering bar), limit at ask, cancel if unfilled in 5 s, no chase. Contract: nearest 0.50Δ, spread ≤ 5% of mid (core) or ≤ 8% at half size (sector), OI ≥ 500, ask size ≥ 10. Kill: 30-session expectancy ≤ 0 or top-3 share > 50%. This is Jeffrey's trade, written down; the Owner Mirror Bot measures how far it is from his actual hand.
- **B. Held-score plus surge (challenger, shadow only).** Core ±2 held ≥ 30 s with a surge in the last 2 min (61.5%, n = 26); light ±3 held 5 min with surge (67.5%, n = 40). Tested with and without the C/P input. It becomes a trade source only if it beats A's base rate; n ≥ 300 before anyone calls it anything.
- **C. ## 10. Risks and failure modes

- **Overfitting and consumed data.** The 10/20 studies were unregistered searches on two correlated sessions with overlapping snapshots counted as n, and the researcher saw everything; those dates are dev-only. Campaign 1 has zero headroom, so every idea here is a campaign-2 registration on dates ≥ 10/21. Mitigation: registration before search, B2 baseline, session-block bootstrap, event-count floors, sealed future dates on `oideval`, and the practical rule that nobody re-tunes thresholds by watching the live scorecard.
- **Concentration.** 89% of Rose's paper net in three tickets; correlated AI names on one release are one bet. Mitigation: per-group cap 2, Correlation Sentinel at 3, top-3 share < 40% as a gate, shared-shock report in the scorecard, expectancy reported per opportunity not per book.
- **Regime.** All evidence is from an AI up-tape; bearish-extreme calibration is already inverted; puts have never been traded in paper. Mitigation: no live Stage 2 before the shadow book has traded through at least one −5% QQQ week; validation windows must include ≥ 5 down sessions; setup D registered from the start.
- **PDT and margin.** FINRA replaced the pattern-day-trader designation with an intraday-margin standard effective 2026-06-04, with a firm-level transition until 2027-10-20 during which a broker may keep legacy 4-in-5/$25k rules (UNVERIFIED-S). A seconds-to-hours options book is 100% day trades. Mitigation: ask Alpaca in writing which regime applies; until answered, assume legacy, keep equity > $25k in a margin account, and let the Sentinel cap round trips at 3 per rolling 5 sessions. Cash accounts avoid PDT but hit T+1 settlement and cannot hold Level 3 at Robinhood (UNVERIFIED).
- **Latency.** The LLM path is seconds to minutes; the edge may live inside 10 s; emulated brackets add their own delay. Mitigation: nothing in the order path is an LLM; the Gateway runs on the data mini next to the stream; p99 budget 500 ms decision → order-ready and 2 s to broker ack, measured not assumed; the 5× rule rejects any policy faster than the measured path; entry on the bar after the trigger costs some edge and the cost is measured by the Owner Mirror Bot.
- **Fill quality.** Paper at ask-in/mark-out with zero fees flatters everything. Mitigation: paper marks entries at ask + 1 tick and exits at bid; the label is bid-vs-entry-ask; spread caps in the Sentinel; realized slippage reconciled weekly; the per-ticket break-even gate.
- **Execution.** No native options bracket at Alpaca (UNVERIFIED-S); a software stop that fails in a gap is a real loss; emulated OCO can double-fill on reconnect. Mitigation: 1-contract sizing in Stage 2, idempotent intent ids, reconcile-before-send on reconnect, stream-loss flatten, daily loss limit, broker-side close-all from a phone, weekly drills.
- **Vendor quotas and caps.** FlashAlpha 2,122/2,500 by 4:32 PM on a partial wall day, ~2,850 projected on a full one; X pool at $55.72; Alpaca ~1,000 subscriptions (UNVERIFIED); UW 50 EOD rows per contract. - **LLM nondeterminism and prompt drift.** Two Macro definitions already coexist. Mitigation: LLMs outside the order path; typed outputs only; temperature 0 and prompt hashes for anything archived as a feature; weekly replay of identical inputs to detect drift; no prompt edit can change a threshold.
- **Terms of service and licensing.** Robinhood bot prohibition (UNVERIFIED-3P); Massive non-professional plans. Mitigation: bots trade only through brokers whose API terms allow automation; read the agreements before Stage 2.
- **Single points of failure.** One data mini hosts the collector, the page and the Gateway; no network path to the research mini; no off-site copy. Mitigation: nightly archive to the research mini and an encrypted off-site copy; UPS; KILL file and heartbeat; a documented restore drill.
- **Measurement debt.** No NAV, no R, no fees, sign errors, administrative closes mislabeled as wins. Nothing in section 2 advances until the 57 tickets are restated with signed cashflows and fees and the `admin` category is separated.
- **Cost creep.** Run rate today ≈ $99 Alpaca + $299 FlashAlpha Growth + UW (Basic $150 / Advanced $375, plan unrecorded, UNVERIFIED-S) + X + LLM tokens.
